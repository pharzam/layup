# Architectural Vision Brief: Autonomous Multi-Harness Meta-Orchestrator

## 1. Problem Statement & Background

Engineering teams increasingly operate across fragmented, heterogeneous agent harnesses. This setup creates substantial operational friction, high cognitive overhead, and lacks unified orchestration. Key challenges include:

* **Fragmented Execution & Context Drift:** Disparate harnesses execute independently without shared contextual constraints or standardized verification loops.
* **Operational Bottlenecks:** Human operators are repeatedly drawn into low-level execution decisions, turning human intervention into an operational bottleneck rather than a strategic governance layer.
* **Absence of Systematic Conflict Resolution:** When reasoning agents disagree or stall, pipelines either halt entirely or produce brittle execution loops lacking objective, evidence-based mediation.
* **Poor Observability & Traceability:** Multi-agent workflows often lack deterministic telemetry, obscuring token consumption, task latency, and historical audit trails required for systematic policy optimization.

The objective is to establish an autonomous meta-orchestration system that consumes a high-level Problem Statement Brief (PSB) alongside a template-driven governance repository, driving end-to-end product delivery with minimal, governed human intervention.

---

## 2. Functional Capabilities & Requirements

The system must autonomously support the following functional capabilities across the product engineering lifecycle:

### 2.1 Autonomous Specification & Architecture Synthesis
* **Requirement Derivation:** Ingest raw PSBs and derive formal Preliminary Design Reviews (PDRs) and Product Requirement Documents (PRDs).
* **Domain Architectural Alignment:** Synthesize and adapt system architectures to project-specific domain constraints, validating compatibility against established requirements.
* **Phased Implementation Planning:** Formulate structured, milestone-driven execution roadmaps mapping specifications directly to deliverable tasks.

### 2.2 Dynamic Squad Assembly & Cross-Harness Execution
* **Specialized Agent Sourcing:** Dynamically recruit and provision specialized agent squads from registered harnesses, allocating frontier and high-reasoning models based on task complexity.
* **Reciprocal Cross-Verification:** Delegate verification tasks to independent harness instances. Outputs generated by one harness must be strictly validated, audited, and tested by a counterpart harness against governance repository rules.

### 2.3 Milestone Management & Retrospective Feedback
* **Cadence Governance:** Advance autonomously across defined milestones, evaluating deliverable completion against explicit exit criteria.
* **Continuous Organizational Learning:** Execute automated retrospectives at the close of every milestone, committing actionable lessons learned to iteratively refine subsequent execution cycles.

## 3. Architectural Principles & Solution Constraints

The implementation must strictly adhere to the following architectural invariants and operational constraints:

### 3.1 Option-Driven Decision Routing
To maximize reasoning efficiency and cost-performance trade-offs, the orchestration layer must incorporate option-driven decision models (such as Jev and Laya architectures) guided by project Architectural Decision Records (ADRs) and repository governance:
* **Model Routing:** Deterministically map tasks to optimal reasoning tiers and harness profiles.
* **Context Routing:** Selectively scope, prune, and route minimal necessary context windows per agent invocation.
* **Solution Routing:** Systematically evaluate, score, and rank competing technical pathways against architectural constraints.

### 3.2 Scientific Conflict Resolution via Blind Panels
When execution stalls or inter-agent consensus deadlocks:
* **Isolated Fresh Sessions:** The system must spawn a fresh, context-isolated panel of high-reasoning "Blind Agents" detached from prior conversational bias.
* **Hypothesis-Driven Posture:** Panelists must operate with an objective, scientific inquiry mindset—proposing first-principles hypotheses and formal trade-off analyses rather than functioning as adversarial interrogators.
* **De-escalation Protocols:** The panel delivers a synthesized, evidence-backed resolution path to unlock downstream execution.

### 3.3 Reinforcement-Driven Policy Optimization & Feedback Loops
The orchestrator must integrate closed-loop reinforcement learning mechanisms to continuously tune routing, policy selection, and resource allocation:
* **Deterministic Reward Modeling:** Formulate mathematical reward signals that balance objective completion, test pass rates, verification scores, and token/time efficiency metrics.
* **RLAIF & Reciprocal Critic Architecture:** Leverage cross-verification harnesses as automated environmental critics (Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback) to dynamically penalize redundant loops and score solution candidates.
* **RLHF via Governance Interventions:** Ingest human intervention decisions, approval signals, and deadlock resolutions as ground-truth preference data (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) to refine global routing heuristics.

### 3.4 Issue-Centric Interaction & Full Telemetry
* **Unified Communication Substrate:** All inter-agent handoffs, status reports, human prompts, and code reviews must take place exclusively through structured repository issues and threaded comments.
* **Granular Traceability:** Every discrete action must be immutably logged with comprehensive telemetry, including token consumption, wall-clock execution time, and latency metrics.

### 3.5 Human-as-Observer Governance & Circuit Breaking
* **Strategic Oversight:** The human operator acts as a strategic monitor and final authority, retaining approval rights strictly over scope boundaries, budget ceilings, and final milestone/architecture sign-offs.
* **Clean Escalation Gateways:** If autonomous retry budgets, reinforcement exploration limits, and blind arbitration panels fail to break a deadlock, the system must trip a circuit breaker, exit the autonomous loop, and compile a structured diagnostic package for the human operator.
* **External Resolution Ingestion:** The framework must natively allow human operators to consult external models or alternate sources and inject candidate solutions directly back into the execution loop via threaded comments.
