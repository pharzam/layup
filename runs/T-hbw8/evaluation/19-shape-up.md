## Verdict

Borrow the pattern: appetite, the circuit breaker with its "all downhill" extension test, the hill chart as a computed progress signal, rabbit holes and no-gos, and the betting table as one bet per milestone fill real gaps. Reject the six-week and two-week values (no evidence for agents, I4) and "QA and review are not a gate" (conflicts with S7, I5 and Invariant 9).

## What it is

Shape Up is Basecamp's product method. It has three parts. Shaping: a small senior group turns a raw idea into a rough, solved, bounded concept. They fit it to an "appetite" (how much time the idea is worth) and write it up as a "pitch". Betting: before each cycle, a few senior people choose among a few pitches. There is no backlog. Each bet is a fixed time box with a "circuit breaker": by default, no extension. Building: a small team gets the whole project, not tasks. It discovers the tasks, maps them into "scopes", shows progress on a "hill chart" (uphill = unknowns, downhill = execution), and "hammers" the scope to fit the time box.

The book targets one risk: "the risk of not shipping on time" (https://basecamp.com/shapeup/0.3-chapter-01#targeting-risk). It says it is not about "the risk of building the wrong thing". The author splits the book into "basic truths" and "specific practices"; the practices "are scale-dependent" (https://basecamp.com/shapeup/4.1-appendix-02#basic-truths-vs-specific-practices).

Verdict words in this file. `use`: LAYUP can take the concept as a rule with small wording changes. `borrow the pattern`: LAYUP takes the idea, but the mechanism must change for agents. `reject`: LAYUP does not take it. A book has no code, so no concept can be "used" as a program.

## PSB items, decision points and measures it helps

- **S1 Problem Statement Quality, HDP 2, Early Question Share.** Rabbit holes and no-gos give the review a fixed list of four questions. Their output feeds the one batch.
- **S5 Role Handoffs.** Breadboards give a checkable text form for user-facing flows only. Scopes give a shared vocabulary between roles.
- **S8 Human-on-the-Loop, HDP 1, HDP 3, Task Intervention Rate.** Appetite at Intake and one bet per milestone, each a one-screen brief. "Uninterrupted time" matches the rule that other human input is unplanned.
- **S9 Stall Resolution, HDP 5, Stall Rate, Stall Diagnosis, vision 3.5.** The circuit breaker is the absolute cap. The computed hill position is the progress measure. The "all downhill" test gives the examiner's diagnosis a fixed form.
- **S10 Cost Visibility, Cost per Requirement, Problem 5.** Appetite gives a budget number that exists at Intake. The cap works in wall-clock where tokens are not reported (O-80).
- **S11 Specification Synthesis.** Shaping at the right level of abstraction, and the pitch's five sections as the frame of a design brief. It does not help the fact trace.
- **HDP 4 Escalations.** Scope hammering draws the line: task-level cuts are the squad's; a cut of a requirement or its criterion escalates.
- **First-Review Acceptance.** "Get one piece done" gives the idea owner an early working slice. "Compare to baseline" (https://basecamp.com/shapeup/3.5-chapter-14#compare-to-baseline) gives the acceptance brief a question: is it better than what the user has now?
- **Vision 2.2 (model allocation by complexity).** Bet size and hill position give a class: uphill to the reasoning tier, downhill to the execution tier.
- **Vision 2.3 (milestones and retrospectives).** Cool-down gives the phase between milestones: retrospective, rule batch, next bet.

## Deep-check findings it helps

- **Author-7, Fable-M11 (budget per task before tasks exist).** Appetite at Intake. Milestone budgets set one bet at a time inside the band. No per-task budget, because tasks are discovered.
- **Sol-11 (money stop cannot compute a total).** Partly. A wall-clock cap from the appetite still stops work when tokens are unknown. It does not give the money total.
- **Sol-22 (a new record resets the stall clock).** The computed hill position: only fewer open unknowns or more passed tests of the frozen set count as progress. The circuit breaker is the absolute cap that the fix asks for.
- **Fable-M4, Author-1 (disagreement loop is never a stall).** A ping-pong round moves no hill position, so it stalls. Findings outside the acceptance criteria and against no-gos are nice-to-haves by default.
- **Fable-M5 (the clock fires on waits).** Partly. "Getting oriented" is the same class: a normal phase with no output. It gets its own allowance. The wait for a human needs the fix that Fable-M5 states.
- **Fable-M6 (stall with no diagnosis).** Partly. The extension test forces a per-scope uphill/downhill diagnosis before any branch.
- **Fable-M19 (a wrong gate stops the milestone with no exit).** The circuit breaker ends the milestone at the cap, so the retrospective runs.
- **Fable-M16, Sol-23 (no complexity input for routing).** Bet size class and hill position.
- **Sol-19, Author-18 (code sets `Must`).** Scope hammering separates requirement priority (idea owner) from task must/nice (squad).
- **Author-19 (architecture approval each milestone).** One bet comment per milestone with a one-screen brief.
- **Approval-load problem (no human reviews a 2,000-line diff).** The pitch form as the one-screen brief.
- **Sol-7, Fable-M7, Sol-14 (human load from shadow mode and fallbacks).** None. Shape Up has no rule for model decisions.
- **Sol-12, Author-5, Fable-M21, Sol-17, Sol-18, Sol-20 (telemetry and trace).** None.

## Where it does not fit

- **It is written for human teams.** Its motives are human: "feel the deadline looming", morale, "code monkeys", "Nobody says 'I don't know'". Agents have none of these. The mechanisms that rest on a human feeling must become computed checks (I6).
- **Every number is a human value with no evidence for agents (I4).** Six weeks, two weeks of cool-down, one to two weeks for a small batch, three days of orientation, three to five chowder items, two cycles of cleanup. LAYUP can keep each one as a parameter with no default until a pilot measures it (O-79, O-82).
- **Agents are fast and cheap for each attempt.** A cap in weeks means nothing. The cap must be in money, tokens and wall-clock, from the O-68 band.
- **The PSB decision points are fixed.** The book's betting table sets intent and can drop a project. In LAYUP the PSB and the idea owner set intent. The default of the circuit breaker must be "stop and escalate or reshape", not "cancel".
- **Self-reported status.** The hill chart and the must/nice mark are team judgement. For LAYUP they are self-declaration unless code computes them from records.
- **QA and code review as optional.** This conflicts with S7, I5 and Invariant 9. LAYUP keeps the gates.
- **No backlog and decentralized lists.** This conflicts with I1: memory outside Git is not a record.
- **Tool coupling.** Appendix 1 (https://basecamp.com/shapeup/4.0-appendix-01) ties hill charts and pitches to the Basecamp product. LAYUP takes only the ideas, as records in Git and issue comments.

## Limits of this evaluation

- The source is a book, not software. Nothing was installed or run, and no claim here was tested on a target. Each mechanism above is a proposal for the rewrite plan, not a result.
- The site shows no edition and no date. The page text can change without notice. The pages were read on 2026-09-29.
- Images (sketches, hill chart screenshots, pitch examples) were not read. Their captions were read. The text alone was enough for each concept above.
- The site reserves all rights. This file uses short quotes only. LAYUP documents must not copy the book's text.
- The file `operator-decisions.md` in the brief holds O-76 to O-86; O-68 is quoted here as the task states it, not from its source record.
- The "computed hill position" (tests frozen = top of the hill) is this evaluator's mapping. The book does not state it. A pilot must show that it detects real stalls and does not fire on normal work.
